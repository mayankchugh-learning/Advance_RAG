Singapore Attractions Notes

Gardens by the Bay is the one attraction that shows up on every list, and it earns the spot. The Supertree Grove is free to walk through during the day, and the nightly light show (Garden Rhapsody) is also free - two shows a night, check the schedule since times shift. The paid Cloud Forest and Flower Dome conservatories are worth the ticket price, especially Cloud Forest with its indoor waterfall.

Marina Bay Sands is technically a hotel/casino/mall complex, but the SkyPark observation deck on top is a separate ticketed attraction with the best skyline view in the city. The infinity pool is hotel-guests-only, a common point of confusion for visitors who think they can buy a day pass to swim in it - you can't, only stay guests have pool access.

Sentosa Island covers a lot of ground beyond just Universal Studios - S.E.A. Aquarium, the cable car over from Mount Faber, several beaches (Siloso, Palawan, Tanjong), and Fort Siloso if you're into WWII history. It's connected by a short causeway or the Sentosa Express monorail from VivoCity.

The Merlion statue is smaller in person than photos suggest, and mostly a photo-op stop rather than something you spend real time at - fine to combine with a walk along the Marina Bay waterfront rather than a dedicated trip.

Chinatown, Little India, and Kampong Glam (with the Sultan Mosque) are the three heritage districts, each with a distinct character and worth a half-day each rather than rushing through in one afternoon. Kampong Glam specifically has a strong concentration of independent cafes and street art alongside the historic mosque.

The Singapore Zoo and River Wonders (formerly River Safari) are out near Mandai and easily combined into one day trip - the Night Safari is also in the same complex if you want to do a day visit followed by the night version.
