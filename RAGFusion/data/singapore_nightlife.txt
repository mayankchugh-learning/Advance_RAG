Singapore Nightlife Notes

Singapore's nightlife scene is more varied than people expect for such a small country. Clarke Quay is the most touristy strip - riverside bars and clubs like Zouk (which moved here a few years back) draw a younger crowd, lots of EDM and hip-hop nights on weekends. Cover charges can be steep, S$20-40 depending on the night and whether there's a guest DJ.

For something quieter, the rooftop bars are worth the markup. Ce La Vi and 1-Altitude both have skyline views over Marina Bay, though 1-Altitude is technically the highest alfresco bar in the world. Go right at sunset if you want photos before it gets crowded. Smoke & Mirrors at the National Gallery is less known and has better cocktails for the price.

Night markets aren't really a "nightlife" thing here the way they are in Taiwan or Thailand, but the pasar malam (temporary night markets) pop up around HDB estates and are worth seeking out for cheap street food and a completely different vibe from the clubs - kids, families, elderly folks playing chess, alongside the food stalls.

Bars in Chinatown and Tanjong Pagar tend to be more low-key - craft beer places, wine bars, speakeasies hidden behind unmarked doors. Employees Only and Jigger & Pony are the well-known speakeasy-style spots if you want a proper cocktail rather than bottle service.

Cultural shows are a different kind of "night out" - the Wings of Time show at Sentosa is a light and water show, touristy but genuinely well done. Marina Bay Sands also does a free light show (Spectra) every night that's worth catching if you're in the area anyway.

One thing to know: Singapore has strict laws about public drunkenness and noise after certain hours, and drinking on the MRT is banned (not just discouraged - actually banned, with fines). Bars generally close by 2-3am on weekdays, later on weekends in licensed zones like Clarke Quay and Orchard Towers.
