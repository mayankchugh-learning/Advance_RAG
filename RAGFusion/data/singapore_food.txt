Singapore Food Notes

Hawker centers are the heart of eating in Singapore, and honestly some of the best food I've had here has cost under S$5. Maxwell Food Centre near Chinatown is famous for Tian Tian Hainanese Chicken Rice - there's almost always a queue, but it moves fast. Lau Pa Sat is more touristy and open-air, satay street outside at night is the draw there.

Chili crab is the dish everyone tells you to try, and it lives up to it - go to a proper seafood restaurant rather than a hawker stall for this one, since it's a whole ordeal involving bibs and getting messy. Jumbo Seafood and Long Beach are the well-known chains, though locals will point you to smaller independent places too.

Laksa, char kway teow, and Hokkien mee are the noodle dishes to know. Katong Laksa specifically is a regional variant worth seeking out - richer coconut broth than other versions. Char kway teow is best from stalls that still cook it over a proper wok with real heat - you can taste the difference from a place using an electric hob.

Breakfast culture here revolves around kaya toast, soft-boiled eggs, and kopi (local coffee, often served very sweet with condensed milk unless you specify otherwise - "kopi o" for black with sugar, "kopi c" for with evaporated milk). Ya Kun Kaya Toast is the well-known chain if you want a reliable version without hunting for a specific stall.

Little India and Chinatown both have their own distinct food identities worth exploring separately from the general hawker center scene - proper Indian thalis and dosas in Little India, and a wider range of Chinese regional cuisines (not just Cantonese) in Chinatown beyond the usual chicken rice.

One practical note: most hawker centers are cash-only or have inconsistent card acceptance stall to stall, so carry small denominations.
